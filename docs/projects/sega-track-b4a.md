## 0. Where it stands

* **Every static picture rule that the random-scene differential could find is now identical with BlastEm**: 637 scenes (27 seeded random-scene sets and 3 probe sets, `Workshop/MdGolden/md_scene_sets.tsv`) were pixel-identical with the oracle's screenshots when they were pinned; 264 of them were the sets the rules were derived from, 224 more came from fresh seeds that were never looked at before the rules were final (224 of 225 identical, the one other scene is 4 pixels off, see 6), and 72 from fresh seeds of the round before the last (sprites 30 of 30, interlace 2 30 of 30 without a change; the attribute cache family 11 of 12, the miss gave the H32 attribute wrap), and the last 40 (fresh seeds 74 and 77 of the `all` family, 20 scenes each) were identical the first time they were looked at. `scenes-check.ps1` re-verifies all 637 without BlastEm in 2 minutes.
* The vertical interrupt is right (it was 780 master clocks late), and so are the VBlank/HBlank flags, HINT, the Z80 interrupt line length and the V counter step in both widths.
* **DMA throughput matches the oracle within 0.2 percent** in both widths and every kind (68000 to VRAM, fill, copy, display on and off) after three fixes found with new probes (`dma_rate.py`): fills and copies stepped at the wrong slot (a 2048-byte fill took one line, the oracle takes ten), blanked H40 lines have six refresh slots not twelve, blanked H32 lines five not four (section 1.1 and 7); and a fourth, found because a golden pin had been re-recorded blind: a DMA into CRAM or VSRAM (one slot a word) loses the slot three ticks after each refresh slot, 198 words a blanked H40 line (section 9). **The Chilly Willy direct colour demo is pixel-identical with BlastEm** (6 of 6 screenshots).
* **A freeze**: a DMA copy or fill started in the last 56 clocks of a blanked line froze the VDP for good (D&D - Warriors of the Eternal Sun at its frame 541, on main too); found by the library census, fixed, with a check that fails without the fix (section 7).
* **Sprites** (section 1.4): BlastEm caches Y, size and link of each sprite as the table is written; the sprite's Y has 9 bits outside interlace 2; an H32 link list follows links up to 79. The scene family `satcache` (a table loaded while the table address register names another place) went from 0 of 12 scenes identical to 12 of 12; Nemesis' sprite masking ROM (H32 stage) is now a golden entry, oracle-identical.
* **Where a mid-frame vertical scroll write takes effect** (section 11): a full-screen vertical scroll entry is taken once a line, near its end, for the next line, and the 2-cell column scroll reads its entries about 20 pixels ahead; found with Road Rash II, probed with `vsmid.py`. Road Rash II replays 5 of 9 pictures exact against the oracle (3 before) and **OutRunners' 2-player split screen 9 of 9 (2 before)**. The vertical interrupt is the same on a PAL and an NTSC machine (Overdrive 1 frame 1007 is not a PAL VINT error).
* **The HINT latch** (section 11): the horizontal interrupt due at the end of a line is decided one line earlier by R0 bit 4 (sample at clock 2855 of the line before, H40): a HINT that comes due while HINT is disabled is lost (B4b's probe), one decided while enabled is kept while the game disables HINT and enables it again in the vertical blank (OutRunners, Super Hang-On). Super Hang-On and Sonic 2's two-player split screen replay **9 of 9 pictures exact** against new oracle captures, OutRunners 9 of 9. A HINT-timed vertical scroll probe (`vsh.py`, write time controlled to 2 CPU cycles, dots from the colour RAM as a clock) confirms the column read lead and the full-screen latch on the oracle. The oracle's CPU data port writes with the display on run 0.07 to 0.14 percent (4 to 6 words a frame) faster than ours: the narrowest known cause of the CRAM flicker phase.
* Open, with data: the 68000 takes an interrupt later on the oracle after long instructions (section 4; not mine to change), Titan Overdrive 1 frame 1007 and Overdrive 2 frame 3709 (section 5), and the list in section 6.

## 1. The rules, exactly (for whoever implements them elsewhere: `mix/MdToNes.cs`, the plugin, a future core)

Line coordinates: master clocks from the line's H counter 0 (3420 clocks a line in both widths; the H counter's top byte advances every 16 clocks in H40 and every 20 in H32 up to the sync stretch).

### 1.2 Planes (`MdVdpRender.cs`)

**2-cell column vertical scroll** (R11 bit 2). The columns are pairs of the plane's cells, so their boundaries follow the plane's horizontal scroll, and the stretch before the first boundary is a special case.
For plane P (A: entries `VSRAM[2c]`, B: `VSRAM[2c+1]`) with its horizontal scroll `hs` on that line (the 10-bit value of the line's hscroll entry), `vsMask` = `0x7FF` in interlace mode 2, else `0x3FF`:

```
b0 = hs & 15                                   (screen x of the first pair boundary)
if x < b0:          H40: vs = VSRAM[2*19] & VSRAM[2*19+1]   (the last pair's two entries ANDed; the same for plane A and plane B)
                    H32: vs = 0
else:               column = (x - b0) >> 4 ;  vs = VSRAM[2*column + (P == B ? 1 : 0)]
                    the column ends at x = b0 + 16*(column + 1)
vs &= vsMask
window at the left (plane A only; the window covers x < W = 16 w, w >= 1) with b0 != 0:
                    for W <= x < W + b0:  column = w (the entry of the pair AFTER the one that would be there), not (x - b0) >> 4 = w - 1
```
(the old rule, `column = x >> 4`, is the case `hs & 15 = 0` of this). Proved by the probe scenes `vsprobe` (5 scenes: H40 and H32, hscroll 0 and 13, left column blank), `vsprobe2` (12 scenes: fine scroll 1, 3, 5, 7, hscroll 29, H32, plane B, left column blank) and `vsprobe3` (12 scenes: a window of 16, 32 or 48 pixels at the left with hscroll 0, 5, 13, 21), made by `oracle-tools/md-picture/vsprobe.py` (`python vsprobe.py out.bin [2|3]`): plane A or B only, every plane row identifiable from the pixels, a different VSRAM value per column; and by the random families `vsc` (20 scenes) and `vscw` (with the window, 20 scenes). All of them are pixel-identical with BlastEm's screenshots. The H32 stretch value 0 was the last piece (probe 1's values, A15 AND B15 = 64, showed 0); the 11-bit scroll was found by a random scene with a 32 x 128 plane in mode 2 (53,000 pixels off with ten bits, 15 with eleven, 0 after the sprite rule below).

**When a vertical scroll write takes effect** (section 11 has the probe and the numbers). Full-screen mode: `VSRAM[0]` and `VSRAM[1]` are taken once a line, at clock `VsFullLatchH40` = 2855 (H32: `VsFullLatchH32` = 3000) of the line, and are that scroll for the NEXT line; a write earlier in a line shows from the next line, never on the line it is made in, a write after the clock misses the next line too. Column mode (R11 bit 2 set): each column's entry is read about 20 pixels ahead of the pixel the column starts at (`VsramExtraLead`, 20, on top of the 16 pixels of the index stage), so a write that many pixels before a column's start is too late for that column. `--slot-set vslatch=0` (old: live value) and `vslead=0` switch them off.

**Plane sizes that do not fit.** R16 width code `ws` and height code `hs` (0: 32, 1: 64, 3: 128 cells): `if ws == 3 and hs != 0 then hs = 0` (128 wide is only 32 high), `else if hs == 3 and ws != 0 then hs = 1` (64 wide with the height code 128 is 64 x 64). 32 x 128 is valid. Proved by the scene set `sc_sizes` (family `sizes`, seed 31, 16 scenes: 11 identical before, 16 of 16 after) and `sc_plain` scenes 16, 25, 29.

**Interlace mode 2** (R12 bits 2-1 = 11): cells are 8 x 16 (a name table entry's pattern is 64 bytes: address `tile * 64 + row * 4`, rows 0-15, vertical flip 15 - row), the field's line `y` is picture row `2y + field` (field = the odd-frame flag), vertical scroll (11 bits) and sprite Y (offset 256, not 128) count picture rows, the window's cell row is `(2y + field) >> 4`. BlastEm's screenshot in this mode is **one field** (320 x 224 or 256 x 224, 240 high in V30), not a woven picture; ours matches that field pixel for pixel (every interlace-2 scene of the pinned sets: 24 + 29 of 30 + the interlace-2 scenes of the other sets). `MdVdp.Picture` stays the field; `MdVdp.WovenPicture` (448 or 480 rows) is the extra: the app (`MdSession`) shows the woven picture at `DisplayWidth` 640 in mode 2, the field's own picture in mode 1 (no double height there: the lines of the field are the picture lines).

**Left column blank (R0 bit 5) in shadow/highlight mode** (oracle: `sc_shadow` scenes 0, 8, 16, 17, `sc_spr` 1, `sc_plain` 21, `sc_plain2` 12 and 26 were 900 to 3700 pixels off; now identical). Without shadow/highlight the first eight pixels are the backdrop and nothing else changes. With it:
1. the blank column (x 0-7) is the backdrop **in shadow** (always, a high-priority tile hidden under it does not make it normal);
2. the **planes** (A, window, B) in the second cell (x 8-15) are the planes of the first cell (x 0-7), i.e. the composite of cell 0's planes with cell 1's sprites; the sprites stay where they are. This second part looks like an oracle quirk (hardware would hardly do it) but no hardware measurement says either way; it is behind `MdVdp.LeftBlankShadowRule` (3 = both, 1 = only the first, 0 = the old picture; `--slot-set lbshadow=N`).

### 1.3 Shadow/highlight sprite rule (full)

State of a pixel (R12 bit 3 set), in this order, for every pixel:

1. `tilePrio = (plane A tile priority bit) OR (plane B tile priority bit)`, taken from the tiles even where they are transparent there. `state = normal` if `tilePrio` else `shadow`.
2. The plane ladder picks the plane pixel: a visible (colour != 0) high-priority A pixel, else a visible high-priority B pixel, else visible A, else visible B, else the backdrop. `aHi`/`bHi` = a visible high-priority pixel exists in A/B.
3. A sprite pixel (the first one drawn on that pixel; colour != 0) **wins the ladder** if its priority bit is set, or if there is no visible high-priority plane pixel (`!(aHi || bHi)`). A sprite that does not win does nothing at all (no pixel, no operator).
4. A sprite that wins:
   * palette 3 colour 14 (`0x3E`) is the highlight operator: the pixel keeps the plane colour and `state` becomes normal if it was shadow, highlight if it was normal; palette 3 colour 15 (`0x3F`) is the shadow operator: `state = shadow`; neither draws itself;
   * any other sprite pixel is drawn with its own colour; its brightness is normal if its priority bit is set **or its colour index is 14** (any palette but 3), else `state` (so a low-priority sprite is in shadow over low-priority planes, and normal where a transparent high-priority tile made the state normal). **Colour 15 outside palette 3 follows the state like any other colour** (the first version of this record said "14 or 15"; 1,700 oracle pixels of colour 15 over shadow were all shadowed).
5. Output colour = CRAM entry in normal, shadow (levels 0-7 of the DAC: 0 27 49 71 87 103 119 130) or highlight (levels 7-14) brightness.

Old rule for comparison: operators acted whatever the priorities; a low-priority sprite was hidden whenever `state` was normal, even over a low-priority plane; colours 14/15 were shadowed. Proved by the scene sets `sc_shnw` (family `shnw`, seed 32, 16 scenes: shadow/highlight only, planes full size 0-1, full scroll: 50-440 differing pixels per scene with the first rule, 13-75 with the second, **0 with the third**: colour 15 follows the state), `sc_shadow`, `sc_spr`, the fresh-seed sets, and the probe `shprobe.py`, by the `--md-scenes --sprstat` statistics (what brightness BlastEm gives a sprite pixel, by sprite priority, colour class and plane A/B state) and `--classify` (the same-entry mode changes).

### 1.4 Sprites: the attribute cache, the Y bits, the link list (`MdVdp.cs` `SatNote`/`SpriteCache`, `MdVdpRender.cs` `BuildSprites`)

BlastEm does not read a sprite's Y, size and link from VRAM when it draws the line: it keeps them in a cache that is fed as the table is written.

```
R5 table   = (R5 & (H40 ? 0x7E : 0x7F)) << 9          (the register's value at the moment the byte reaches VRAM)
every byte written to VRAM at address a (data port, DMA from the 68000, fill, copy; a FIFO write counts when it executes):
    rel = (a - table) & 0xFFFF
    if rel < 0x280 and (rel & 7) < 4:   cache[(rel >> 3) * 4 + (rel & 7)] = byte       80 sprites x 4 bytes: Y high, Y low, size, link
a sprite's attributes (tile, palette, priority, flips) and its X are read from VRAM at table + 8 * i + 4 .. 7 when the line is drawn
```
So a sprite table that was loaded while R5 named another place is not in the cache (the cache holds whatever was last written into the range `R5` names, or zero), and a game that writes its table with R5 naming it (all of them) sees no difference. Both widths cover 0x280 bytes = 80 sprites.

* **Y has nine bits** outside interlace mode 2 (`Y & 0x1FF`, screen line = Y - 128), ten bits in mode 2 (`Y & 0x3FF`, picture row = Y - 256). Found with the cache scenes, whose cached Y words are random (bit 9 set half the time).
* **Link list**: start at sprite 0, follow `link & 0x7F`, stop at link 0 or at a link of **80 or more**, in both widths; at most 80 (H40) or 64 (H32) sprites are walked. An H32 list goes **through** sprites 64 to 79 (the cache covers them): their Y, size and link are the cache's, but their **position and attributes are read from the table modulo 512 bytes** (`a = table + (8 * i & 0x1FF)`): sprite 78 is drawn where sprite 14 says, with its tile and palette (H40: no wrap, the table is 640 bytes). Two observations fix this: Nemesis' sprite masking ROM in H32 (sprite 63 links to a sprite 64 whose X is sprite 0's, off the left edge: nothing shows; a first version that read sprite 64's own attributes drew it at x 232, y 112 and was 122 pixels off, found when the ROM was put into the golden file) and the second seed of the attribute cache scenes (a link to sprite 78: the oracle draws a 3 x 3 sprite with sprite 14's X and palette 3; ours, with sprite 78's own bytes, drew it 77 pixels to the right; a first rule that drew nothing at index 64 and up fixed the ROM and missed this scene).
* Everything else about sprites is as in 1.3 and `sprite_isolate.py` / `--sprline` (per-line limits, masking, X = 0).

Found by the scene family `satcache` (`scenefuzz.py --family satcache`: the registers are loaded with R5 naming a random other table, the 64 KB of VRAM, CRAM, VSRAM and the real table written, R5 set to the real table last): scenes 0 of 12 identical before the cache (70,000 pixels each), 9 of 12 with the cache and `Y & 0x3FF` (the other three 462, 615 and 109 pixels, all at the left edge of the picture), 11 of 12 with nine Y bits, **12 of 12** with the H32 link list (and the masking ROM stays identical with it: 0 pixels differ). The second seed (72) of the family found the attribute wrap above: 11 of 12 before it, 12 of 12 after; the fresh seed 73 of the sprite family (30 scenes) and 76 of the interlace 2 family (30 scenes) were identical without any change. Variants tried: caching the whole 8 bytes (X and attributes too: no change on the three scenes, so not that), a cache of 0x200 bytes in H32 (scene 3 stays off: h32links=0), reading the table from VRAM (satcache=0). Pinned as set `f_satcache` (12 scenes, seed 71); each rule fails its pins when switched off (`scenes-check.ps1 -Sets f_satcache -SlotSet satcache=0` 0 of 12, `spritey9=0` 9 of 12, `h32links=0` 11 of 12) and `--md-pic-test` has a check for each (137 checks; `h32wrap=0`, which reads the H32 sprites at 64 and up from beyond the table, fails the check for that, f_satcache2 and the masking ROM's golden entry).

For an implementer of another core: keep the cache as above, and when a tool sets VRAM directly take the cache from the table (`MdVdp.RebuildSpriteCache`, called by `ResetPictureState`).

The drift-sampling method: the 68000 meets the frame 5 master clocks later in phase each frame (896,040 mod 7 = 5), so a probe that repeats one thing every frame samples an event at every phase; the event's time falls out of how many samples saw it one step late (calibrated on our own emulator, whose times are known), and the HV reads of the same stream put the status samples on the line. The oracle's answers come out through the quick-save state's work RAM (`BlastEm F7 pause, backtick save`) or its screenshots.

The scene method, and how the rules were found: a random scene is a complete VDP state shown for 12 frames; a rule is wrong where the oracle's screenshot and ours differ. `--classify` says what kind of difference each pixel is (same CRAM entry in another brightness, another entry), `--all` dumps our plane A, plane B and sprite values next to both colours of every pixel in a window, and a search of our layers for the oracle's row finds the rule (the left-blank quirk showed up as "the oracle's x 8-15 equal our plane B at x 0-7"). Probe scenes (one plane, every row identifiable by its colours) then fix the exact formula. Lost Start presses in BlastEm shift the pairing of shots and scenes (probe 3: two lost presses; the tool cannot tell), so every new set is looked at for a run of large differences before it is believed.

BlastEm takes an interrupt later than we do after most instructions that are not a nop. A main loop of one instruction kind plus `bra.s`, HINT every line, mean handler entry time after the HINT time (20,000 interrupts each; `probes_b4a.LOOP_BODIES`, `hlb.py`, ours then the oracle):

| `move`: two `move.w (a6),d1` | 462.3 | 470.3 | +8 |

The oracle's longest latency in the movem pair is 212 cycles against a 158-cycle loop (an interrupt that arrives during a long MOVEM waits for the end of the NEXT instruction). The pattern (zero for nops, growing with an instruction's length and with its write cycles, slightly negative for two shift/multiply loops) says the oracle samples the interrupt line at a different point of the instruction than our `BOARD_MD.BeginAccess` does (ours: the start of the last bus access); `BOARD_MD.IplSampleAdjustClocks` (`--slot-set iplshift=N`) moves ours by a fixed amount and cannot fit it (a fixed shift cannot be +91 for movem and 0 for nop). For B4b: the rule needs the instruction kind (the position of the last bus cycle).

## 7. Found with the new probes: the slot search, the refresh slots, a freeze

**The freeze.** The library census (section 8) stalled on D&D - Warriors of the Eternal Sun: `--md-run` hangs at its frame 541 on main as well. A debug check in the VDP's wait loops found the loop: `MdVdp.RunTo` with `dma = Copy`, `Time` exactly at a line's end. `NextFreeSlot(from)`, when its search goes on in the next line, set `from = ls` (that line's start) instead of keeping its own `from`; a copy or fill that starts within 56 clocks of the end of a line has `from = dmaStartTime` in the next line, and the search returned a slot at the start of that line, **before** `from`: `ConsumeSlot` did nothing (the engine had not started), the next search returned the same slot again, and time never advanced. Fixed for the fill and copy steps (`DmaStepWork`); `--md-test` has a check that starts a copy at every clock of the last 70 before a line end in a thread with a timeout (it fails with `--slot-set nextslotfix=0`).

**What else the same search did.** For fill and copy the old search handed out slots before `from` at every line start, so a fill of 2048 bytes in a blanked H40 line took one line (`dma_rate.py`: 239.65 laps a frame against the oracle's 25.24; fills and copies 5 to 15 times too fast, the 68000 to VRAM transfer was right to 2 percent). The oracle's DMA durations then showed the refresh slots: blanked H40 lines have 204 free slots, H32 166 (section 1.1). The ordinary FIFO writes and data port reads keep the old search (`NextFreeSlotMode = 2`; `--slot-set nextslotfix=1` applies the fix to them too): with it 21 roster golden frames changed, because the old search's extra slots at line starts are part of what the fitted timing needed; those paths were not re-fitted.

**Why the golden frames moved.** A fill of 64 KB at boot (Sonic 1 does two) now takes the 320 lines the hardware takes, so every roster game's boot is 1 to 5 frames longer, and the same pictures appear that many frames later. `--md-golden --shift` shows it: all 21 moved checkpoints were found again at +1 to +5 frames with the pinned hash unchanged, so the oracle-verified pictures are still produced; the checkpoints were moved (`--move-shifted`). One frame between them (Sonic 1 frame 89, the white flash after the SEGA logo) does not appear at all in the new sequence and is not a golden frame; it was not compared with the oracle (a capture of Sonic's boot frames was queued).

**H32 flicker.** See section 6: the last-line dots are matched (identical picture at a matching frame), the frame-to-frame drift is not.

## 9. DMA from the 68000 into CRAM and VSRAM, and the direct colour demo

**What was wrong.** The golden entry "Chilly Willy direct colour DMA" was only a regression pin (it hashes what the emulator draws), and when it was re-recorded after the slot refit of section 7 nobody looked at the picture: it had turned into horizontal smears. The demo (`dmacolor7`, source in `md-roms/dmacolor7-src.zip`) turns the display off, sets the CRAM auto-increment to 0 and DMAs 198 x 224 words into CRAM entry 0, so the backdrop colour at each pixel is the CRAM word written in that slot: **exactly 198 words a blanked H40 line** (the author's number, tuned on a real console), and the oracle's screenshot shows a clean picture. Matching each picture line against the bitmap's words (for line L, the offset b that makes pixel x equal the colour of word `L*198 + b + x/2`) shows b constant over the lines on the oracle (no drift) and drifting by the difference of the line lengths on ours (204 words a line after the refit: a smear; 192 with the old twelve refresh slots: milder).

**Measured with `cram_rate.py`** (DMA of 32, 128, 512 and 2048 words from ROM into CRAM and into VRAM, laps per frame, H40 and H32, display on and off; same method as `dma_rate.py`, same ROM source and poll loop):

|---|---|---|---|

| CRAM, H32 blanked, 2048 words | 20.136 | 20.718 (0.9719) | 20.122 (1.0007) |

The oracle's slope for a one-word-a-slot transfer, from the 128 and 2048-word laps, is **197.8 words a blanked H40 line and 161.2 in H32** (the 204 and 166 free slots of section 1.1 minus 6 and 5: one more slot lost for each refresh slot), with the same intercept as ours (0.30 line); the two-slot-a-word VRAM transfers, fills and copies lose nothing extra and run at the 204 and 166. The old twelve-slot table (six pairs, the second slot of each pair three slots after the first) was right for CRAM and wrong for everything else; the refit of section 7 was right for everything else and wrong for CRAM. Short transfers (32 and 128 words) are still 0.5 to 1.5 percent faster on ours than on the oracle (about one to two slots a DMA), and display-on H32 CRAM at 128 words 1.4 percent; not chased.

**The rule.** In a blanked line (display off, or a line past the picture) a word moved by DMA from the 68000 into CRAM or VSRAM (one slot a word) cannot be written in the free slot **three ticks after each refresh slot** (`SlotTable.AfterRefresh`, `MdVdp.DmaRefreshBubble`, `SlotTable.BubbleOffset = 3`; `--slot-set dmabubble=0` and `bubbleoff=N` for experiments). The offset was found by trying it against the demo's screenshot: offsets 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 leave 2,418, 1,198, 1,170, 2,346 and 3,542 pixels wrong, **3 leaves none**. Words written by the CPU through the data port, VRAM DMA, fills and copies are untouched (CPU writes to CRAM in blanked lines were not measured: `wr_rate.py` on the oracle was queued twice and BlastEm gave no data both times).

**Start delay.** The first fetch of a DMA now waits 78 master clocks after the command (it was 56, fitted with the old slot table). The window the demo allows is 72 to 84 at least (60 to 68: every line one word off, 37,800 pixels; 88: off the other way). Inside it the oracle-pinned Overdrive frames pick the value: Overdrive 1 frame 786 passes at 56, 64, 70, 74, 78, 80, 82 and fails at 72, 76, 84; Overdrive 2 frame 3709 fails at 74 and 80. 78 and 82 satisfy all three (70 passes both Overdrive frames; the demo was not run at 70); 78 is the middle of the window. (That such tiny changes flip a whole demo frame says the DMA's start is still not modelled exactly: a 16-clock quantum of the start would explain a lot; the numbers above are the data.)

**The direct colour demo is pixel-identical with BlastEm** (6 screenshots, 0 differing pixels each, at 100 to 600 steps; our frames 10, 101 and 400 equal the oracle's picture) and is a golden entry marked `verified: oracle`. `--md-test` has two new checks (a 4,000-word DMA into CRAM in a blanked H40 line takes 20.0 to 20.6 lines, 19.54 without the rule; the same transfer into VRAM is not slowed), and `--slot-set dmabubble=0` fails the first and the golden entry.

**Streets of Rage, and what it says about the fills.** B4b's replay of a captured Streets of Rage (REV 00) run (15 pictures, the default pad script, 1,500 steps) was 15 of 15 identical before the slot refit and is 8 of 15 after it (exact to step 800, 26 pixels at 900 where a small particle sprite is one frame off, wrong from 1000). `--slot-set nextslotfix=0`, the old fast fills, makes it 15 of 15 again; the picture scan then finds the oracle's pause picture at our boundary 392 against 395 with the fills fixed (the 64 KB fills of the boot now take 1.2 frames each; Streets of Rage does four in its first 1,000 frames). Streets of Rage polls the status register until each boot fill is done (the VDP trace shows no port access between the fill's start and its end), so it does nothing during a fill that could cost time; so the oracle's boot should be as long as ours and the 3 frames the fast fills 'saved' are not a property of the oracle. The rules:
1. **Full-screen vertical scroll (R11 bit 2 clear): the entries VSRAM[0] (plane A) and VSRAM[1] (plane B) are taken once a line, near its end, for the NEXT line.** A write never shows on the line it is made in, whatever its time in the line, and shows from the next line on for a write up to about H counter top byte 142 in H40 (a write at 145 first shows on the line after next); in H32 the threshold is at about 126 to 130. Ours: `LatchVscrollIfDue` takes `Vsram[0..1]` when the line's clock reaches `VsFullLatchH40` = 2855 or `VsFullLatchH32` = 3000 (any time from the last free slot of the picture to the first pre-slot of the next line fits the probe: H40 2608 to 2911, H32 2960 to 3180; the values are the start of the horizontal blank and just after the first pre-slot), `TakeLineVscroll` at the start of the next line makes them that line's entries; a write after the threshold misses that line too. `DrawScrollRun` uses the latched entries (`vsLatchA/B`); tools and state loads reset the latch to the current VSRAM (`ResetVscrollLatch`; a state saved at a frame boundary is unaffected since the latch is taken again before the next picture line).
2. **The 2-cell column scroll reads each column's entry earlier than we did**: the oracle's thresholds (H counter top byte of the last write that still reaches the column) are 55 for the column at pixel 160 (column 10) and 109 for the column at pixel 272 (column 17), ours were 10 and 15 H counter steps later (2 pixels a step). `MdVdp.VsramExtraLead` (default 20 pixels, `--slot-set vslead=N`) renders the line up to the write's pixel plus the render lead plus this lead before the new entry is stored, which agrees for column 10 and leaves column 17 about 5 steps (10 pixels) late: not calibrated better than that (the two columns suggest the lead is not exactly constant).

**Shinobi III (roster 13): exact for 300 steps, then falling leaves at other positions: the game's random number is advanced by its idle loop, so it measures the timing of the whole frame; game side, not a VDP rule.** With the corrected start the pictures are exact to step 300 and differ from 321 on by a constant 112 to 118 px (leaf sprites; the first difference was at 301 in the oracle's own snapshots). Snapshots of two runs (`shinsnap6`/`7`): the saved states equal ours to 0 to 2 work RAM bytes up to step 296 (snap 296: one byte, `$FF001C`), at 300 32 bytes differ and the set stays at about 40 bytes up to 400: the word `$FF001C` and two lists of leaf objects (`$FF4237-42A5`, `$FFF539-F547`). `$FF001C` is the game's random number; it is a 16-bit register that the idle loop of the main program (`$512-$520`, the loop that waits for the vertical interrupt: 4 instructions, `pc $518` writes it) rotates once an iteration, about 8 to 9 iterations a line, so about 400 master clocks an iteration (between 55 and 60 68000 cycles); the loop runs between the end of a frame's work and the interrupt, so **the random number at the next interrupt is the number of idle iterations since the work ended, modulo the period**: it is a counter of the frame's remaining time. Ours and the oracle have the same value at step 295 (RAM 3 bytes apart, the register one rotation: ours `$3E` against the oracle's `$1F`, one iteration of the idle loop), the leaf spawn code (`pc $9704`, steps 298 to 299) uses the register, and from there the leaves are at other places. So the two machines are one idle iteration (about 400 master clocks, 0.1 percent of a frame) apart in the time they spend before the interrupt, accumulated over the 700 or so frames since power-on (the register keeps rotating from frame to frame): under one master clock a frame, far below anything the picture shows, and not a VDP rule; the loops that decide it are the same port and DMA timings B4b is fitting (a handler with two 68K-to-VRAM DMAs of 320 and 512 words a frame, the 68000 resumed after each: a 16 clock difference in the release already moves the idle phase). One caution about captures: a plain or PauseSnap run of the oracle can lose a frame-advance tap (a second run of the same snapshot list, `shinsnap8`, has every frame counter of the game one lower than ours at 296 to 300 where the first run had them equal): the tool says so when the picture of a shot matches an earlier one of ours; a capture should be made twice before a difference is believed.

**Strider (roster 5, the lead's item 3): the first picture difference is a one-frame phase, and the phase comes from what a 32-bit write to the VDP costs the 68000. Handed to B4b (the board); no VDP rule involved.** The capture is `strsnap` (default pad, PauseSnap: the start boundary is chosen from the work RAM) plus plain shots `strshots`.
* **What differs.** Before the intro the pictures are identical (steps 120 to 145). From the first intro frame on, the oracle's picture at step n is exactly ours at step n+1: `regcmp.py` (every oracle picture against every one of ours, above and below row 150) gives **0 differing pixels** for oracle steps 197 to 201 against ours 198 to 202, stars and sorceress alike. The 13 to 14 work RAM bytes that differ in the snapshots are the stack page below the stack pointer (stale register values pushed by the interrupt handler, present already in the pause-point state) and a frame counter that is one apart.
* **Snapshot semantics (needed to read the states).** `oraclerun -Snaps` saves the state of step n at the START of the n-th advance (after n-1 advances) and takes the screenshot after it; so the state of snap n corresponds to ours at step n-1 (the numbers of this paragraph are with our replay started at the scanned boundary itself, as it was done before the start correction above; with the correction ours' steps are numbered one lower and nothing in the argument changes, as Strider does not depend on the start; found by matching RAM and VRAM: the oracle states of the snaps 129 to 143 match ours at 128 to 142 to within 8 to 24 VRAM bytes, the ones of 146 to 148 match ours at 146 to 148, the jump is the phase). The PC in a state is at the first main-loop instruction after the interrupt handler returns, about line 230 of our timeline (our replay stops at line 232).
* **Where the frame is gained.** After the Start press at step 120 the game's handler reads the pad (line 225), the main code switches the display off at step 121 and runs two VRAM clears with `move.l d1,(a1)` then `dbf d7,-4` (pc $22DD2, a1 = $C00000: 5,646 iterations and 11,291 data port writes a frame for about 6 frames), then a ~19-frame data decompressor (pc $205A0 to $205FA: a bit-unpacker writing 430 bytes of tile data a frame through the data port; while it runs the interrupt handler finds the main loop busy and only counts the frame), which ends at line 238 of frame 145 in ours (after the interrupt of line 224: the next interrupt finds the main loop waiting and starts the scene one frame later). The oracle's decompressor is a constant 9 to 14 lines ahead of ours from its first frame on (VRAM progress of snapshots 129 to 143 against ours: +16, +12, +8, +20, +14, +17, +20, +24 bytes of 430 a frame; it does not grow), so it finishes before the interrupt of frame 145 and the scene starts a frame early. The decompressor itself runs at the same speed: its instruction mix as lap loops on both machines (`looprate.py`, bodies dec, shifts, tbl, rom, alu, 4 laps of straight-line code, master clocks a lap ours / oracle: 5,395.42 / 5,396.73, 2,320.46 / 2,321.03, 1,010.75 / 1,011.00, 782.98 / 783.17, 1,807.96 / 1,808.40) is +0.024 to +0.025 percent slower on the oracle each, which is the known 0.02 percent difference of the refresh model (B4b's `timerefresh`) and nothing else. So the lead is in the display-off clears of the first half second.
* **The cost of a data port write, measured (master clocks a lap of 16 writes, H40, ours / oracle; `looprate.py` bodies `vdpw`, `vdpwl`).** `move.w d0,(a3)`: display off 1,124.64 / 1,121.35 (-0.21 clock a write), display on 3,757.00 / 3,752.50 (-0.28); `move.l d0,(a3)`: display off 1,580.19 / 1,564.68 (**-0.97 a long write**), display on 6,270.40 / 6,236.92 (-2.09). Strider's clear is the long write plus a taken `dbf`: about 1 clock an iteration, 5,646 iterations a frame, 6 frames: the 9 to 14 lines. Test with a throw-away experiment (a refund of k master clocks per data port write access, not committed): the end of the decompressor moves from line 238 of frame 145 (k = 0) to line 231 (k = 0.3), line 223 of frame 145 (k = 0.5) and 6 of 6 pictures at steps 197 to 202 are exact from k = 0.5 up (0 of 6 below; the 6 of 6 is also reached by the other route: `timerefresh=1;refreshstall=12`, a 68000 0.22 percent faster overall, which breaks other things). A flat refund breaks two oracle-verified golden entries (k = 0.5: md05 Strider frame 1597, and the Chilly Willy direct colour DMA demo at frames 10, 101 and 400, which turns static), so the rule must depend on the instruction's shape (word or long, display on or off); that is B4b's board work (`BOARD_MD`, the VDP bus cycle), who has the numbers, the test case and the replay command.
