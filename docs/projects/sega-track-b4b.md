## What BlastEm was asked, and what it answered

* **Does a vertical interrupt that came due while the VDP had it disabled stay pending?** Fatal Rewind enables it seven frames after the display came on and our 68000 takes it at once (the handler then jumps through a vector the game has just cleared). Five probe variants (`probes_b4b.vint_enable`): enable right after the VBlank flag rose, with the status register read twice, 40 lines into the display period, with a mask raise and lower in between: **BlastEm keeps the interrupt pending and takes it the moment it is enabled, status reads included, exactly as we do** (variant A, enabling before the interrupt is due, differs by B4a's fitted VINT time). So this is not Fatal Rewind's cause by itself (see its own section: later probes found a difference in the horizontal interrupt, fixed by B4a).
* **How many instructions run after the mask is lowered before a pending interrupt is taken?** `move.w #$2000,sr`, `move.w d6,sr`, `andi.w #$F8FF,sr` followed by `addq`, `nop`, `mulu`, `movem`: **right after the mask instruction, 30 of 30 frames each, BlastEm and ours alike.**
* **Where does it take an interrupt relative to the instruction that is running** (B4a's table, section 4 of `sega-track-b4a.md`: nops equal, movem +91 clocks, jsr/rts +65, clr.l +38, movem pair +283)? Three measurements, all against BlastEm 1.0.0 with HINT every line (R10 = 0) and a loop of one instruction kind and a `bra.s`:
  1. **Mean entry time** (`iplloops.py`, 10,000 interrupts; BlastEm minus ours, master clocks): move +6, divu +29, mulu +48, mixed -1, movem pair +262, movem out/in +74/+73, movem d0-d1 +54, jsr/rts +56, clr.l/add (a6) +33, tst.l +15, move.w/l to memory +25/+26, lea/pea +17, mulu1 -18, divu1 +13, lsl8 +22, lslr -19 (B4a's own run of the same idea: section 4).
  2. **Instruction lengths** (`run_timing.py`, the V counter over 40 and 160 lines of straight-line copies, so the reads' own cost cancels): **identical on both machines to the master clock** for nop, `tst.b ($8000).w` (12.30 cycles), `move.b d1,($8000).w`, `move.w (a6),d1`, `btst #3,d1`, `cmp.w (a6),d1`, `move.l (a6),(a5)` (20.50), `clr.l (a6)`. The gaps are therefore not lengths.
  3. **Return-address histograms** (`run_rethist.py`, 4,000 interrupts per loop and machine; the share of interrupts that return to the `bra.s`, i.e. were taken after the body): ours (old rule: the lines are sampled at the start of the last bus cycle of every instruction) against BlastEm: NOP 0.286 / **0.789**; `btst #3,d1` 0.400 / 0.747; `tst.b ($8000).w` 0.545 / 0.782; `clr.l (a6)` 0.667 / 0.581; `lsl.l #8,d5` 0.117 / 0.338; `movem.l d0-d7,(a6)` **0.879 / 0.122** and `movem.l (a6),d0-d7` 0.883 / 0.120 (BlastEm takes the interrupt that arrives during a MOVEM *after the branch that follows it*); `jsr sub1` (`sub1: rts`) bra 0.346 / 0.434, i0 0.217 / 0.358, inside the subroutine 0.437 / 0.208.
  Solving the share and the mean entry time together for the one-body loops (`meanfit.py`; the two numbers fix the two sample points of the loop): **BlastEm's sample for a taken `bra.s` lies about 1 cycle after the instruction starts** (0.1 to 1.4 over five loops; ours was 6, the start of its last bus cycle), for NOP, `lsl.l #8` and MOVEM the body's sample is also about 1 cycle after its start (2.0, 1.6, 0.6 to 0.9), and for `clr.l (a6)` / `add.l d3,(a6)` it is 8.8 cycles after the start (ours 16). The remaining memory-access loops (`move.b d1,($8000).w`, `cmp.w (a6),d1`, `move.l (a6),(a5)`, `tst.b ($8000).w`) put the sample at 0 to 12 cycles after the start, where the old rule puts the start of the last bus cycle 4 to 4.5 cycles later: the share depends on the instruction's bus cycles in a way that one offset does not fit (the sample is on a bus-cycle boundary within about 1 cycle in every case, but not always the last one).
  **What was adopted** (`M68k.BranchIplMask = 7`, `M68k.MovemFreezesIpl = true`, both default; slot-set `braipl=`, `movemipl=`): a taken BRA/Bcc/DBcc/BSR, JMP/JSR and RTS/RTR/RTE take their interrupt sample where they start (`IplFromStart`, the level the VDP drives at the instruction's first clock), and MOVEM in either direction stops sampling after its first bus cycles. Distance to the oracle (sum over 17 loops of half the L1 distance of the histograms): **4.83 with the old rule, 3.41 with the branch rule, 1.75 with both** (`rh_score.py`); NOP + BRA.S 0.714 (BlastEm 0.789, the old rule 0.286), `jsr/rts` 0.436 / 0.346 / 0.218 (BlastEm 0.434 / 0.358 / 0.208), MOVEM out 0.122 / in 0.120 (BlastEm 0.122 / 0.120). Pictures: **identical** hashes with and without the rule on Strider, Sonic 2, Streets of Rage 2 at frames 400, 800, 1,200 (`--md-run --pic-hash-at`, slot-set `braipl=7;movemipl=1` against none; the 68000 state hash of SoR2 differs, as it must), and all 16 roster captures replay with the same exact-picture counts (differing pixels move by -12 to +78: Shinobi III +46, Comix Zone +31, Hard Corps -12, Castlevania: Bloodlines +78, noise at the level of the ordinary variation); `--md-golden` 75 of 75 (the rule does not move Streets of Rage frame 245, unlike `IplMode = 1`, which stays off). `--md-test` has the loop check with the old rule shown to fail (`InterruptSamplePoints`: NOP + BRA.S share must lie in 0.66 to 0.84 and the MOVEM loop in 0.08 to 0.18; the old rule gives 0.29 and 0.88).
  **What the table implied** (B4a section 4): "nops equal" was right for the *mean* but the histogram of the same loop differs (0.286 against 0.789): two sample points per loop with opposite errors cancel in the mean, so a mean-only fit cannot find them; "a fixed shift cannot be +91 for movem and 0 for nop" is exactly the MOVEM-and-branch rule above (predicted +95 for the MOVEM loop from the windows, measured +91); "the rule needs the instruction kind" holds for the memory-access instructions, which are the open part (above, and the next steps).

## Fatal Rewind (Killing Game Show), what was known before wave 5

**Solved in wave 5 (section 3 below): the hypothesis at the end of this section (BlastEm goes on after a double fault) is wrong; the game does not double-fault on the oracle, an interrupt-acknowledge rule explains it.** The measurements below stand.

Ours halts (a double fault) at frame 314 with no input. The sequence on our side (`--md-run --trace --trace-pc`, `--ram-watch`): the ROM checksum loop (frames 2 to 56, the code at `$7FFC0`), VDP set-up with the vertical interrupt enabled and the display off at frame 59, a 64 KB VRAM clear by the 68000 (frames 59 to 62), the Electronic Arts logo animation (frames 65 to 307), then at frame 307 the new-game init: the handler cell `$FFE03E` gets `$28B74`, `TKGS` is written at `$FF0000` (a warm-start marker), the cell is cleared by a 13,932-iteration clear loop, the vertical interrupt is switched off (R1 `$44`), and **at frame 314, line 255 (inside the VBlank), the game lowers the 68000's mask, rewrites R0 to `$14` (horizontal interrupt on) and R1 to `$64` (vertical interrupt on) and jumps to a routine at `$2B79C` whose fifth instruction stores the handler's address in the cell (`$2B7BE`)**. Ours (before B4a's HINT latch rule) took the horizontal interrupt that was pending while it was disabled right after the jump, the vertical one after the first handler instruction, and the vertical handler's `jsr ($E03E)` goes to address 0: address error while stacking, double fault. **BlastEm does not crash: its game goes on, and its RAM at the same point shows the cell set to `$28C04` (the value `$2B7BE` writes).**

What was measured on BlastEm to find the difference (probes in `oracle-tools/md-picture/probes_b4b.py`, `run_b4b.py`, `run_irqdelay.py`; the 68000's mask is 0 in all, display on):

* A vertical interrupt that came due while it was disabled and is enabled later **fires, after one NOP, exactly as in ours**: enabled right after the VBlank began (A), with status reads before it (B, E), in the display period 40 lines later (D), held for 1, 3 and 7 whole frames while disabled (G1, G3, G7), 8, 21, 26 and 30 lines into the same VBlank (LE8, LF0, LFA, LFE), after three held frames with R0 rewritten first (X3): all identical. Enabled all along with the mask at 7 and lowered later: the same; with the enable bit cleared and set again (pM), R0 and R1 rewritten (pN), six status reads (pO): the same.
* **A horizontal interrupt that came due while it was disabled and is enabled in the display period (probe H): ours takes it after one NOP (we keep it pending); BlastEm takes its first one more than 150 NOPs later (more than a line and a quarter): BlastEm does not keep a horizontal interrupt that came due while it was disabled.** That is a real difference and Fatal Rewind meets it (ours takes the horizontal interrupt first and the vertical one right behind it), **but it does not explain the game by itself**: with R0 then R1 written as the game does it (probe W) and with the status register read all through the VBlank before the enable (probe R; the probe HV, which also keeps a horizontal interrupt pending and lowers the mask just before, took no interrupt in the 150 NOPs on either machine: a fault of the probe, not a result), BlastEm takes the vertical interrupt **one NOP after the R1 write, like ours**, and in the game that is before the routine at `$2B79C` has stored the handler's address, so BlastEm's 68000 also reaches the vertical handler with the zero cell. What it does from there is not known: its RAM in a no-input timeline (one state a second, `fr_timeline.py`) shows the same sequence up to the clear, then `TKGS` and the cell alternating between written and cleared for four seconds and the game back on its feet with the cell at `$28C04`, which looks like a restart through the game's own exception vectors.
* A 68000 double fault: our 68000 stops (as the hardware does); the BlastEm window did not answer the oracle's key presses afterwards (probe `run_dfault.py`: its script timed out), which its Fatal Rewind run never showed. **Hypothesis, not tested further:** BlastEm's 68000 does not stop on the address error that follows the jump to address 0 and the game's vectors restart it; a real console would be halted, so the game must not get there on hardware, and the next steps are a double-fault probe whose capture does not need the window to answer (BlastEm's own VGM log or a RAM state taken before the fault and one after) and a read of the game's address-error vector.

B4a took the horizontal-interrupt finding (probe H) and made it a rule in `MdVdp.FireHint` (d8136af: a HINT is latched by R0 bit 4 as it was at clock 2855 of the line before, `hintEnCur`/`hintEnNext`; the replay counts of all 20 roster games are the same before and after it). **Fatal Rewind is not changed by it either**, checked on the merged build: the exception counts and the halt are identical with the old rule, the new rule and the enable-at-due-time rule (`--slot-set hintlatch=0|1|2`: vectors 3 x1, 11 x1, 28 x1, 30 x1 and `the 68000 HALTED (a double fault)` each time). R0 stays `$04` (horizontal interrupt off) until the jump in frame 314 and `$14` after it, so the pending-while-disabled case is real but the game's crash does not depend on it. The open question stays the one in the hypothesis above: BlastEm's 68000 goes on after the jump to address 0 (its game reaches the cell value `$28C04`), and ours stops as a real 68000 does.

### 3. Fatal Rewind / Killing Game Show: explained and fixed

The earlier hypothesis (BlastEm continues after a double fault) was wrong: BlastEm hangs on a real double fault (the probe `run_dfault.py` makes its window unresponsive; its address errors work: `run_afault.py` gives the same four address errors and one line-F as ours), and **the oracle's game never double-faults**. BlastEm's RAM and registers frame by frame (`snaprun` 30 advances at the crash, PC from the register block of the state) show the game running normal code ($224DE, $27F4A, $28C42) and, after 20 seconds, "FOR THE GREATEST SHOW ON EARTH". What differs is the order of two interrupts. The game enables the horizontal interrupt (a stale one is still pending from frame 307) and the vertical one in two writes, then jumps to a routine whose fifth instruction stores the handler's address in the cell the vertical handler jumps through (empty after the RAM clear). `run_fr.py` reproduces the init in a probe (variants E, F, L, A): on both machines the CPU takes the horizontal interrupt first (the vertical enable is not yet visible at its sample); **ours then nests the vertical interrupt in the horizontal handler and jumps through the empty cell (double fault); the oracle never takes the pending vertical interrupt in the routine that follows** (probe L: not within 300 NOPs; it comes at the next VBlank, when the cell is filled), whether the horizontal handler is long or a bare `rte`, and it does take it at once when no horizontal interrupt is involved or when the horizontal one was handled 60 NOPs before the enable (`run_vq.py` 1-6 and `run_fr.py` G, H, J, M all agree with ours). **The rule** (`MdVdp.HintAckDropsVint`, static, `--slot-set hintackvint=0`): acknowledging a horizontal interrupt drops a vertical interrupt that is pending and enabled. Result: `exceptions taken 28x1 30x985` in 1,300 frames, no address error; frame 1,800 shows the Psygnosis logo, frame 3,400 the game ("Push A to fastforward"). Two md-test checks (the second able to fail). B4a's `HintKeepNonzero` (a horizontal interrupt that came due while disabled stays pending for R10 != 0) answers variant A of the probe: with both rules in the tree A, E and F match the oracle in every field (first HINT with 0 instructions, VINT return address at the spin loop).

### 8. BlastEm's quick-save state is not a fixed layout (the RAM offset moves)

Every RAM comparison used the work RAM at `$10941` of the state file. The state is 141,640 bytes plus 12 per entry of some section in front of the RAM (it looks like the VDP's write FIFO; sizes seen in the 124 states of the captures: 141,640, +12, +24, +36, +48, and +15 and +63 for two games with a cartridge section after the RAM), and the RAM sits that much later. A comparison at `$10941` in such a state reports thousands of differing bytes that are not there (Comix Zone after advance 759, section 4; any of B4a's Strider snapshots taken with a longer state: his sizes were 141,652 and 141,664). **Fixed in the tools:** `MdOracleCli --ramcmp` finds the offset by agreement with our RAM (it says `(the state's layout is shifted ...)`), `--scan-state` does the same for the pause state (it holds a copy of our RAM at every boundary and takes the shift with the best agreement), `oracle.py state_ram` places the RAM by the size. Probe results that read RAM out of a state (`rate.py`, `run_vq.py` ...) used states of the base size except three VDP-write loop probes (`rate_vdpw_*`, one state each, +12): they are not in the numbers of this record. Statements of the first version of this record that rested on the fixed offset are corrected in sections 4 above and in the roster table.

## Decisions

* The 68000's interrupt sampling follows BlastEm's histograms for taken branches/jumps/returns and for MOVEM, **default on**, because no roster picture, no golden frame and no test moved and the distance to the oracle fell from 4.83 to 1.75; `IplMode = 1` (sample at every instruction's start) stays off because it moves the oracle-pinned Streets of Rage frame 245.
* My `RunTo` livelock guard was dropped for B4a's root-cause fix; `NextFreeSlot` is the only protection and the five hung games run (above).
* The live sound swap is mine (`TrySwapCore` Apu branch); `SwapRefusal` (C4b's) answers only for ids the Genesis does not have or units whose state layout cannot take the Z80's, as `TrySwapCore` returns false only then.
* A NES chip unit that is loaded takes the clock of the stock unit it wraps and spends the chip's start-up silently (`MdSoundOnNes.LoadState`, `MdSoundUnit.Clock`): without it a live swap handed the reader all the sound since power-on.
* Status "playable" in the dashboard means: the 15-picture lock-step against BlastEm identical, or identical but for one or two pictures that are an animation or blink phase (listed per game); it says nothing about later levels.

* **Wave 5:** the start of a bus cycle to the VDP window is floored to a 4-clock grid (`BOARD_MD.VdpQuantum`, default 4) with `DmaStartDelay` 72 and `DmaReleaseLatency` 12, because it fixes Strider's intro (6 of 6) and no roster picture, golden hash or test is worse (the golden checkpoints moved one frame earlier with their hashes unchanged); `timerefresh` (the oracle's real refresh) is not adopted because the direct colour demo does not tolerate it. A hack of the same size would be a refund per data-port write; the grid is the smallest rule that fit more than one loop.
* **Wave 5:** acknowledging a horizontal interrupt drops a pending enabled vertical one (`MdVdp.HintAckDropsVint`, B4a's file, one line; `hintackvint=0` turns it off), because it is what the oracle does in Fatal Rewind's init and in no other scenario probed.
* The double fault stays a halt (the oracle hangs on one as well; an attempt to reset the 68000 on it was written, found unnecessary and removed).
* **Wave 5:** the replay stops where BlastEm's frame advance stops (NTSC 231, PAL 247, each less 200 clocks) instead of at 232, because a probe that polls the pad on BlastEm says so and the roster counts agree (16 identical games instead of 14 and Streets of Rage 3 14 of 15 instead of 12); the PAL 224-line probe value (239) is not used because the one game that switches between 240 and 224 lines needs 247 in both.
* **Wave 5:** when one write changes SDA and SCL together, SCL falling is applied first and SCL rising last (`MdEeprom.Drive`), because a game cannot mean a START by the value 0 it writes after an acknowledge clock and the oracle's file for NBA Jam EU proves the other order wrong; the table says College Slam and Frank Thomas are 24C64 because of their two-byte word addresses and, for College Slam, the oracle's 8,192-byte file. NBA Jam TE is left at 24C08 on the strength of its own traffic although the oracle's file differs.
